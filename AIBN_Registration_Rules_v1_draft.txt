This document is the "AIBN Registration Rules" cited as [AIBN-RULES]
in the urn:aibn namespace registration. Terms defined there — work,

Operator — are used here with the same meaning.


1. About this document
----------------------

This document states the procedural rules under which the Registry
Operator assigns reg-segments, under which holders record AIBNs, and
the operational commitments of the Registry Operator. It does not
restate the definition of the namespace; that is the role of the
namespace registration, and of the Metadata Schema for field
definitions.

The namespace document set consists of three parts:

  - the urn:aibn namespace registration (the foundational
    specification: purpose, syntax, scope, identifier persistence,
    resolution);
  - these Registration Rules (assignment and administration);
  - the AIBN Metadata Schema [AIBN-SCHEMA] (metadata fields and
    controlled vocabularies).

Where these Rules and the namespace registration could be read to
conflict, the namespace registration prevails on matters of namespace
definition (scope, syntax, persistence, resolution); these Rules
prevail on matters of procedure.

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
"SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
"OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in
BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all
capitals, as shown here.

The stable URL of this document (header) must be in place before the
namespace registration is submitted.


2. Definitions used in these Rules
----------------------------------

  - Applicant: a natural person or legal entity applying for a
    reg-segment.
  - Contact channel: a private, verifiable means by which the
    Registry Operator can reach a holder (section 4.3).
  - Recording: the entry of a pub-segment and its metadata into the
    AIBN database.
  - Lookup API: the public HTTPS service that accepts an AIBN and
    returns the public metadata record (section 11).
  - Bulk export: the published snapshot of the public database
    (section 11.3).
  - Public record: the metadata of an AIBN as returned by the lookup
    API and included in the bulk export — every recorded field except
    the contact channel and fields marked non-public (section 6.5).
  - Record change log: the append-only list of metadata corrections
    kept for each AIBN record (section 5.5).
  - Database change log: the append-only list of database events
    (recordings, status transitions, corrections) published as part
    of the bulk export (section 11.3).

All other terms are used as defined in the namespace registration.


3. The registry at a glance
---------------------------

  - The Registry Operator is the sole authority for reg-segments: it
    assigns them on application, guarantees their global uniqueness,
    and never reassigns a reg-segment — including after a holder
    becomes inactive or dissolves. The assignment function is not
    delegated to third parties. The values "example", "test",
    "invalid", and "reserved" are permanently reserved and are never
    assigned.
  - Within its reg-segment, a holder assigns pub-segments and
    records them in the AIBN database (section 5).
  - The Registry Operator operates the AIBN database, the lookup API,
    and the bulk export (section 11), maintains the escrow and
    succession arrangements (section 14), and maintains the
    Registration Rules and the Metadata Schema (section 16).
  - The authoritative database (lookup API and bulk export) is the
    sole validity oracle for AIBNs.


4. Obtaining a reg-segment
--------------------------

4.1 Who may apply. Any natural person or legal entity may apply.
Assignment is open: it is not conditioned on copyright subsistence,
publication status, nationality, or membership in any organization,
and an applicant need not be the author or rights holder of the works
it intends to identify (see the namespace registration, Openness and
criteria, and Non-effect on rights).

4.2 How to apply. An application is submitted by email to
[application email address — to be completed] using the application
form published at [application form URL — to be completed]. The
application states:

  - the applicant's name and legal form (natural person or legal
    entity, e.g., a company with its registration number);
  - a postal address (a business or postal address is accepted; it is
    stored as private data and is not published);
  - the contact channel (4.3); and
  - a statement that the applicant accepts these Rules.

4.3 Contact channel. The contact channel is an email address
controlled by the applicant. It is verified at application time by a
confirmation message, remains private, and is never published or
included in the bulk export. The holder MUST keep the contact channel
functional and MUST report a change of address within 14 days. A
holder MAY designate a secondary contact channel for continuity.

4.4 Verification and assignment. The Registry Operator verifies the
completeness of the application and the functionality of the contact
channel, then assigns the reg-segment. Complete applications are
acknowledged within 7 business days and decided within 30 calendar
days. The applicant does not choose the reg-segment string: segments
are opaque codes with no embedded meaning, and requests for
particular values are not accepted. Assignment of a reg-segment
conveys no right in, or endorsement of, the holder or its works.

4.5 Refusal. The Registry Operator may refuse an application only
where: (a) required information is missing, or verification of the
contact channel fails; (b) the application contains knowingly false
information; (c) the applicant is a person whose reg-segment was
terminated under section 9 (Termination of a reg-segment) within the
preceding 24 months; or (d) assignment is prohibited by applicable law
(for example, sanctions rules). A refusal is reasoned and communicated
through the application channel and may be appealed (section 12).

4.6 Changes affecting a holder. The holder MUST report, within 30
days, changes to its legal name, legal form, or address, and MUST
report dissolution or cessation of business. A reg-segment survives a
change of legal form if the holder continues to exist in a successor
form (for example, a sole proprietorship converted into a private
company) and the change is reported; the segment is otherwise not
transferable. A holder may surrender its reg-segment at any time by
notice; its records persist (section 8).

4.7 Multiple reg-segments. An applicant may hold more than one
reg-segment; each application is assessed under 4.4 and 4.5 on its
own. Common reasons are separately administered catalogs (imprints,
divisions, product lines), recording workflows run by different
teams, and separation of an acquired or migrated catalog. Each
reg-segment of the same holder is governed independently:
obligations, notices, removal, termination, and appeal run per
segment, and the refusal window of 4.5(c) applies per person.
Holding multiple reg-segments conveys no priority, quota, or
endorsement; larger volumes are accommodated under 5.3, not by
multiplying segments.


5. Recording AIBNs
------------------

5.1 What may be recorded. A pub-segment is recorded for a single work
as defined in the namespace registration (Purpose, "The concept of a
work"). Whether a creation is a work, and at what granularity, is
declared by the holder under the good-faith representation of
section 9; the Registry Operator does not verify eligibility,
authorship, or ownership. Multiple AIBNs may be assigned to the same
work by different parties; the Registry Operator does not adjudicate
such disputes (see section 10).

5.2 Syntax, uniqueness, and timing. Each pub-segment MUST satisfy the
syntax of the namespace registration (Syntax, ABNF grammar) and MUST
be unique within its reg-segment; the AIBN database rejects
duplicates within a reg-segment. The holder MUST record each
pub-segment in the AIBN database at or before its first public use.
The registration date is the date of recording; the Registry Operator
never backdates a record to an earlier public use.

5.3 Rate and batch limits. At launch, a holder may record up to 100
AIBNs per rolling 24-hour period per reg-segment. Larger volumes
(for example, migration of an existing catalog) are accommodated on
request with a brief statement of purpose; a granted elevated limit
is recorded in the database change log and applies until withdrawn.
Breach of the applicable limit is a removal ground under section 9.
The lookup API's own rate limits are documented at the API endpoint
(section 11) and are separate from these recording limits.

5.4 Malformed registrations. Entries that fail syntactic validation
are rejected at recording time and are never recorded. If a record is
later found to be malformed (for example, through a validation
defect), the Registry Operator notifies the holder through the
contact channel and allows 14 days to correct the entry; an
uncorrected record is set to retracted and the event is entered in
the record change log. Malformation is not an abuse ground; section 9
governs abuse.

5.5 Corrections. A holder corrects its record's metadata at any time;
every correction is entered in the record change log with its date
and nature. The identifier itself never changes, and no correction
alters the registration date.


6. Metadata obligations
-----------------------

6.1 Minimum metadata. Each record MUST contain the minimum metadata
set out in the AIBN Metadata Schema [AIBN-SCHEMA], including a work
title, work type, creation date, and a responsible party; the
responsible party is mandatory and is the accountability and contact
point for the record, as defined in the namespace registration.

6.2 Source of metadata. All metadata is as supplied by the holder.
The registry itself records the registration date and the lifecycle
status of the AIBN; these fields are assigned and maintained by the
Registry Operator and cannot be altered by the holder.

6.3 Controlled vocabularies. Work-type and degree-of-AI-involvement
values MUST come from the controlled vocabularies published in the
Metadata Schema [AIBN-SCHEMA].

6.4 Validation. The Registry Operator validates format and
completeness only. It does not verify authorship, ownership, or
factual accuracy, and states this in all outputs.

6.5 Field-level visibility. Optional fields (for example, AI system
name and version, description) MAY be marked non-public at recording
time. Non-public fields are stored but are not returned by the lookup
API and are not included in the bulk export. The contact channel is
always non-public. The public record is as defined in section 2.


7. Holder obligations
---------------------

A holder MUST:

  - keep the contact channel functional and current (4.3; a dead
    channel is a removal ground under section 9, ground (d));
  - represent the accuracy of its metadata in good faith (6.2;
    section 9);
  - record pub-segments only for works, at or before first public
    use (5.1, 5.2);
  - report identity and status changes (4.6);
  - respond to notices from the Registry Operator within the periods
    stated in these Rules.

Failure to meet these obligations is handled under section 5.4
(malformation) or section 9 (abuse), as applicable.


8. Lifecycle of records
-----------------------

  - active: the default status of a recorded AIBN.
  - retracted: set (a) on request of the holder, at any time, through
    the contact channel, or (b) by the Registry Operator under
    section 9 (abuse) or 5.4 (uncorrected malformation). Retraction
    retains at minimum the identifier, its status, and the
    registration date (see section 13 for privacy limits on what else
    is retained).
  - superseded: set by the holder when a substantially new version of
    the work is registered under a new AIBN; the two records are
    cross-linked through their metadata. Whether a version is
    substantially new is the holder's declaration, guided by the
    namespace registration's Granularity rules.

No status transition ever frees an identifier or a reg-segment for
reassignment. All status transitions are entered in the database
change log and (for records) the record change log.

A reg-segment does not expire for non-use: a holder's ongoing
obligations are those of section 7. A reg-segment ends only by the
holder's surrender (4.6) or by termination under section 9
(Termination of a reg-segment); termination follows notice and the
response period of section 9 and carries a right of appeal
(section 12).


9. Good faith and removal for abuse
-----------------------------------

Good faith. By applying for a reg-segment and by recording each AIBN
in the AIBN database, the holder represents that (a) the recorded
metadata is, to the best of the holder's knowledge, accurate and not
misleading; (b) each registration is made to identify a work as
defined in the namespace registration, and not to interfere with
another party's registrations or to mislead anyone about a work's
origin, identity, or authorship; and (c) the contact channel of
record is kept functional.

Removal grounds. The Registry Operator may set the lifecycle status
of a record to "retracted" (see Identifier persistence in the
namespace registration) where, on documented evidence, it determines
that: (a) metadata was falsified (e.g., a fabricated responsible
party or contact channel, or a knowingly false AI-provenance
declaration); (b) a pattern of registrations has the evident purpose
of interfering with or free-riding on another party's registrations
(e.g., mass duplicate registration of works already registered by
others, accompanied by misleading metadata); (c) registrations
breach the rate or batch limits of these Rules; or (d) the holder's
contact channel has ceased to function and is not restored within
30 days of notice.

Process and limits. Before acting, the Registry Operator gives
notice through the holder's contact channel and allows 14 days to
respond; the decision and its grounds are entered in the record's
change log. An isolated duplicate or erroneous record is not an
abuse ground: the Operator acts on patterns and demonstrable intent,
not on the mere existence of another record for the same work.
These grounds concern conduct toward the registry and its users;
the Operator does not determine authorship, ownership, or
entitlement to register any work, and removal on these grounds is
not a finding that the holder lacked any right to register it.
Disputes between parties remain with those parties under applicable
law. No status transition ever frees an identifier for reassignment,
and nothing here reintroduces ex-ante verification: metadata is
checked for format and completeness only, and these grounds operate
ex post, on evidence.

Termination of a reg-segment. Where the abuse grounds above apply to
a holder's registrations generally (rather than to isolated records),
the Registry Operator may terminate the reg-segment after notice and
the 14-day response period; all records under the segment are set to
retracted and persist under section 8. Termination is entered in the
database change log, is stated as a ground for refusal under 4.5(c)
for 24 months, and may be appealed (section 12).


10. Related records in the lookup API
-------------------------------------

Where the response of the public lookup API for one AIBN indicates
a possible match with another record for the same work — because
both records cross-reference the same external identifier (e.g.,
ISBN, DOI), or share title and responsible party, or share title
and a person identifier (e.g., ISNI, ORCID) — the response lists
the other record's AIBN, registration date, and responsible party,
labeled as an unverified association ("may describe the same
work"). The registry does not rank, merge, or privilege any
record, and the association affects neither the status nor the
validity of either record. Either holder may correct its own
metadata to break a false association; neither may demand removal
of the other's record on the basis of an association alone.
Consumers decide which record to rely on by the identifier cited
by the work itself, the responsible-party field, and
cross-references.


11. The lookup API, bulk export, and mirrors
--------------------------------------------

11.1 Endpoint. The Registry Operator operates one or more public
HTTPS endpoints that accept an AIBN — as a complete URN or as its
NSS — and return the public metadata record. The endpoint URL is:
[lookup API URL — to be completed].
Validation and resolution differ in intent, not implementation: this
is the same service cited under Resolution in the namespace
registration.

11.2 Response behavior. The default representation is JSON-LD;
MARCXML and plain text may be requested through HTTP content
negotiation or an endpoint-local format=marcxml or format=text URL
query parameter (these parameters are not URN q- or r-components).
The JSON-LD schema is published and versioned alongside the Metadata
Schema. Status codes at launch:

  - 200 (OK): record found; the body includes the lifecycle status
    (active or superseded, with the superseding AIBN where set).
  - 410 (Gone): record retracted; the body is the tombstone record
    (section 13.3).
  - 404 (Not Found): no such identifier (never recorded).
  - 400 (Bad Request): the lookup key is not a syntactically valid
    AIBN NSS or URN.

Error responses (400, 404) return a machine-readable problem-details
document. The API's request-rate limits are documented at the endpoint
itself.

11.3 Bulk export. A complete export of the public database is
published as daily snapshots plus an append-only database change
log, at [bulk export URL — to be completed]. The export includes a
checksum manifest for verification.
It contains the public record of every AIBN and excludes contact
channels and non-public fields.

11.4 Mirrors. Third parties may mirror the bulk export and operate
resolvers. A mirror is listed as a recognized mirror when it:
(a) derives its data from the official export and verifies the
checksum manifest; (b) refreshes its data at least every 7 days;
(c) identifies itself as a mirror in its responses; and (d) refers
questions of validity to the Registry Operator's endpoint. The
Registry Operator's endpoint remains authoritative; the current list
of recognized mirrors is published at [mirrors list URL — to be
completed].


12. Appeals
-----------

A holder or applicant affected by a decision under these Rules — a
refusal under 4.5, a removal or segment termination under section 9,
an uncorrected-malformation retraction under 5.4, or a limit decision
under 5.3 — may appeal by notice through the contact channel (or the
application channel) within 30 days of the decision. The Registry
Operator reconsiders and responds within 14 days; where a record is
affected, the appeal and its outcome are entered in the record change
log. Because no identifier is ever reassigned, an upheld appeal
restores the record's previous status without loss of the identifier.
If the appeal is rejected, the decision stands; nothing in these
Rules limits remedies available under applicable law.


13. Privacy, data protection, and retention
-------------------------------------------

13.1 Controller and principles. The Registry Operator acts as data
controller for personal data it processes and complies with the
General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR). Data is
collected minimally, used for operating the registry, preventing
abuse, and meeting legal obligations, and is not sold or used for
advertising.

13.2 What is public and what is private. The public record never
requires a natural person's real name: a pen name or organization
name may be displayed instead. The contact channel, the applicant's
postal address, and fields marked non-public (6.5) are private and
are never included in the lookup API, the bulk export, or the escrow
publication (section 14).

13.3 Deletion requests and tombstones. On a valid deletion request,
personal data is removed from public display; where no legal
retention obligation applies, it is also erased from the operator's
records. The identifier persists with status retracted and a
non-identifying tombstone record: the minimum retained record
contains the identifier, status, and registration date. Titles and
other potentially identifying metadata are retained only where
lawful. This trade-off between privacy and identifier persistence is
deliberate and disclosed in the namespace registration.

13.4 Retention periods. Identifier records are retained for the life
of the identifier (persistent by design). Application and identity
records, and archived contact channels, are retained for 7 years
after the reg-segment ends, for accountability and defense against
abuse. Server logs are retained for 12 months. Retention beyond
these periods occurs only where required by law.

13.5 Data subject requests. Requests for access, rectification,
erasure, or portability are sent to [privacy contact email — to be
completed] and are answered within one month.


14. Continuity: escrow and succession
-------------------------------------

14.1 Escrow arrangement. The Registry Operator maintains a written
data-escrow agreement with an independent escrow agent:
[escrow agent — to be completed]. The deposit is made at least
every 30 days and consists of two layers:
the public layer (the complete public database, including the
database change log, with its checksum manifest) and a sealed layer
(contact channels and other private data), which is never published
and passes only to a successor operator under 14.2.

14.2 Succession plan. The Registry Operator maintains a written
succession plan, reviewed annually, that names the process by which
a successor operator is selected (for example, transfer to a legal
entity or to an established registry organization) and the steps for
handing over operation. The escrow agreement empowers the escrow
agent to act without the Operator's further involvement.

14.3 If operation ceases. If operation ceases with a successor, the
successor continues under the succession plan and the IANA
registration record is updated per the procedures of [RFC8141]. If
operation ceases without a successor, the escrow agent publishes the
escrow public layer in static form so that identifiers remain
verifiable, and the IANA registration record is updated (for
example, to mark resolution inactive). Identifiers remain
syntactically valid URNs regardless of resolution status.


15. Fees
--------

No fee is charged at launch for application, reg-segment assignment,
recording, or use of the lookup API. Any future fee will be
published in a new version of this document at least 90 days before
taking effect, will never apply retroactively to identifiers already
recorded, and will never condition the persistence of recorded
identifiers on payment.


16. Changes to these Rules
--------------------------

The Registry Operator maintains these Rules. Every change is
recorded in the change log below with a version number and effective
date. Material changes — to the assignment mechanism, uniqueness
policy, or resolution requirements — are published for public
comment for at least 30 days before taking effect; other changes are
published and notified to holders through the contact channel at
least 30 days before taking effect. Changes never apply retroactively
to recorded identifiers. If a change affects a statement made in the
namespace registration, the registration record is revised per the
namespace-change procedures of [RFC8141].
