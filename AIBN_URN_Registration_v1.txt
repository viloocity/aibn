URN NAMESPACE REGISTRATION TEMPLATE: AIBN
=========================================

Purpose
-------

This namespace provides persistent, location-independent identifiers (AIBNs)
for works created in whole or in part with the assistance of generative AI
systems, for use by individuals, publishers, and organizations acting on
behalf of such works.

Designation. AIBN expands to AI Bibliographic Name. "Bibliographic" is used
in its information-science sense and covers resources of any medium, not
only text. "Name" rather than "Number" reflects that an AIBN is a structured
name — alphanumeric, variable-length, and assignable by holders as numeric
sequences, mnemonics, or opaque codes (see Syntax) — in keeping with the
"Name" in Uniform Resource Name [RFC8141].

AIBN is a complement to ISBN, not a replacement. ISBN (ISO 2108) identifies
monographic publications at manifestation level and is assigned through the
International ISBN Agency network and the publishing supply chain. Works
involving AI generation can in some channels obtain ISBNs; however, the ISBN
metadata model has no fields for AI provenance (AI system and version,
degree of involvement) or for a responsible human party distinct from an
"author," and not all registration channels accommodate such works. AIBN
provides an identification layer that records this provenance persistently.
A single work may carry an ISBN, a DOI, and an AIBN simultaneously.

Why existing namespaces do not suffice: no existing NID or
persistent-identifier system combines (1) work-level identification, (2)
open assignment to any natural person or legal entity — not conditioned on
copyright subsistence, publisher status, or supply-chain participation, (3)
a metadata model with a mandatory responsible-party field and optional
AI-provenance fields, and (4) URN-framework persistence and resolution
semantics. isbn is manifestation-level and supply-chain-administered; issn
covers serials; DOI, Handle, and ARK are persistent-identifier systems
outside the URN framework with their own assignment policies; urn:uuid
provides uniqueness without an assignment authority, metadata, or
resolution.

Non-effect on rights: An AIBN is a technical identifier. Registration does
not create, imply, transfer, or substitute for copyright, related rights,
moral rights, or legal personality, and does not constitute a determination
of authorship or ownership. The copyright status of a work is determined
solely by applicable law and is independent of AIBN assignment.

Syntax
------

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHOULD, MAY, etc. are to be
interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119][RFC8174].

The Namespace Specific String (NSS) has the following syntax (ABNF per
[RFC5234]):

AIBN-NSS    = reg-segment ":" pub-segment
reg-segment = 1*63seg-char
pub-segment = 1*63seg-char
seg-char    = ALPHA / DIGIT / "-"

ALPHA and DIGIT denote ASCII characters only. Additional lexical constraints
not expressible in ABNF:

  - Each segment MUST begin and end with an alphanumeric character.
  - Assigned identifiers MUST be lowercase. The grammar permits uppercase
    for syntactic compatibility, but no conforming AIBN is ever assigned
    with uppercase characters.
  - Exactly one ":" appears, as the separator; segments never contain ":".
  - The alphabet is a subset of unreserved ([RFC3986] §2.3).
    Percent-encoding MUST NOT appear in any assigned AIBN. Because NSS
    comparison is octet-wise, a URN containing percent-encoded octets (e.g.,
    %61 for a) never matches an assigned AIBN.

Length: The NSS MUST NOT exceed 128 characters. The q-, r-, and f-components
(below) are additional to this limit. Software generating URNs with such
components SHOULD keep the complete URN within 512 characters for
compatibility with common URI-handling implementations.

Component handling:

  - q-component: permitted; not part of AIBN identity; passed to the
    resolution service. The q-component is introduced by ?= under [RFC8141].
    AIBN defines no namespace-specific q-component parameters at initial
    registration. HTTP clients MAY use the endpoint-specific format query
    parameter as described under Resolution; this is a URL query parameter,
    not an AIBN URN component.
  - r-component: introduced by ?+ under [RFC8141]; no namespace-specific
    semantics are defined, and its use is not recommended pending
    standardization of r-component semantics.
  - f-component: as per [RFC3986]/[RFC8141]; interpreted client-side; not
    part of identity.

Lexical equivalence:

  - The NID is compared case-insensitively, per URN syntax.
  - The NSS is compared case-sensitively, octet-wise. Because only lowercase
    is assigned, two URNs denote the same AIBN if and only if their NIDs
    match case-insensitively and their NSSs are octet-identical.
  - q-, r-, and f-components are ignored for identity and equivalence.

Semantics:

  - reg-segment: an opaque registrant code identifying an AIBN holder. It is
    unique across the entire aibn namespace and is assigned by the Registry
    Operator. It carries no embedded meaning.
  - pub-segment: an identifier, unique within its reg-segment, for a single
    creative work (see Granularity, under Assignment). It is assigned by the
    holder, which MAY use numeric sequences, mnemonics, or opaque codes; the
    syntax imposes no scheme.
  - The identified resource is the work itself, not any particular
    manifestation, embodiment, or location of it.
Examples (the reg-segment example is permanently reserved and can never be
assigned):

urn:aibn:example:2026-0001
urn:aibn:example:first-light-7a

Apparent patterns in pub-segments (dates, sequence numbers, words) are
conventions of the holder with no syntax-defined semantics; consumers MUST
NOT parse segments for meaning.

Assignment
----------

Assignment model (hierarchical):

  - The Registry Operator is the sole authority for reg-segments. It assigns
    them on application, guarantees their global uniqueness, and never
    reassigns a reg-segment — including after a holder becomes inactive or
    dissolves. The reg-segment assignment function is not delegated to third
    parties.
  - Within its reg-segment, a holder assigns pub-segments and is responsible
    for their uniqueness within that segment. The holder MUST record each
    pub-segment in the AIBN database at or before its first public use; the
    database rejects duplicates within a segment.
  - The combined NSS is therefore globally unique.

Identifier persistence:

  - An assigned AIBN is never reused or reassigned under any circumstances.
  - If a work is deleted, withdrawn, or retracted, the AIBN remains valid;
    the database status changes (see below) and at minimum the identifier,
    status, and registration date are retained; the title is retained only
    insofar as applicable privacy and other law permits.
  - Metadata corrections are recorded with a change log; the identifier
    itself never changes.
  - Lifecycle statuses: active (default), retracted (withdrawn by the
    holder, or removed by the Registry Operator for policy violation),
    superseded (replaced by a new AIBN for a substantially new version, with
    an optional cross-reference). No status transition ever frees an
    identifier for reassignment.

Granularity: An AIBN identifies one work at creation level. A substantially
revised or new edition SHOULD receive a new AIBN, optionally cross-linked
via supersedes/superseded-by. Minor corrections, formatting changes, and
reissue in a new medium or format (print, EPUB, audiobook, etc.) do not
receive a new AIBN and are not recorded by the registry. (Contrast: ISBN is
assigned per manifestation.) Manifestations of a work are identified by
format-level systems such as ISBN and MAY be cross-referenced in AIBN
metadata; current availability is a property of resolution targets, not of
the registry record.

Openness and criteria: Assignment is open. Any natural person or legal
entity may obtain a reg-segment upon application, subject to published
criteria: (a) acceptance of the Registration Rules; (b) provision of a
private, verifiable contact channel. No fees are charged at launch; any
future fees will be published in advance in the Registration Rules.
Assignment is not conditioned on copyright subsistence, publication status,
nationality, or membership in any publishing organization. Multiple AIBNs
MAY be assigned to the same work by different parties; the Registry Operator
does not adjudicate such disputes, and metadata MAY cross-reference other
identifiers for the same work or party.

Metadata and validation:

  - Minimum metadata at registration: work title; work type (a content type
    from the controlled vocabulary published in the Metadata Schema, e.g.,
    text, image, audio, audiovisual, software, dataset, multimodal, other);
    creation date; responsible natural person or entity; private contact
    channel. Optional: pen name for public display; form of work (e.g.,
    novel, report, image series); language (BCP 47 [RFC5646] tag, e.g., en,
    fr-CA, zh-Hans; more than one MAY be given for multilingual works);
    description (short abstract); creation carrier (MIME type of the form in
    which the work was first fixed, e.g., text/markdown); AI system name and
    version; degree of AI involvement (self-declared value from a controlled
    vocabulary published in the Metadata Schema, e.g., ai-generated,
    ai-assisted, human-with-ai-tools); references to ISBN, DOI, ISNI, ORCID.
    Manifestation data (formats, editions, availability) is intentionally
    absent from the schema; see Granularity.
  - All metadata is self-declared. The Registry Operator validates format
    and completeness only; it does not verify authorship, ownership, or
    factual accuracy, and states this in all outputs. The Registry Operator
    MAY refuse or retract registrations that are malformed or abusive; an
    appeals process is defined in the Registration Rules [URL to be filled].
  - Third-party validation is supported by (a) a public HTTPS lookup API
    returning the public metadata record, with documented rate limits, and
    (b) a complete bulk export of the public database (daily snapshots plus
    an append-only change log). The authoritative database is the sole
    source of truth for validity; this is the mechanism for detecting forged
    or unauthorized AIBNs.

Continuity: The Registry Operator will maintain a data-escrow arrangement
with an independent escrow agent (agent and terms to be published in the
Registration Rules before the registry enters public service) and maintains
a succession plan. If operation ceases without a successor, the escrowed
database will be published in static form so identifiers remain verifiable,
and the IANA registration record will be updated per the procedures of
[RFC8141] (e.g., to mark resolution inactive). Identifiers remain
syntactically valid URNs regardless of resolution status.

Security and Privacy Considerations
-----------------------------------

Security:

  - AIBNs are identifiers, not credentials. They MUST NOT be used for
    authentication, authorization, or access control, and MUST NOT be
    treated as secrets or capability tokens.
  - Implementations comparing AIBNs MUST compare the NSS octet-wise and the
    NID case-insensitively (see Lexical Equivalence). The lowercase-only
    assignment rule eliminates most case-mismatch errors in practice.
  - Identifiers are opaque and MUST NOT be constructed to embed personal or
    sensitive data.
  - The authoritative database (lookup API and bulk export) is the only
    validity oracle; resolvers SHOULD resolve against it or a verified
    mirror.

Privacy:

  - Terminology: "Registry Operator" denotes the URN registrant; "holder"
    denotes a party assigned a reg-segment.
  - The public record never requires a natural person's real name; a pen
    name or organization name MAY be displayed instead. A private,
    verifiable contact channel is required at registration but is not
    published.
  - The Registry Operator acts as data controller for personal data it
    processes and complies with the General Data Protection Regulation (EU)
    2016/679 (GDPR). Data is collected minimally; the retention policy is
    published in the Registration Rules.
  - On a valid deletion request, personal data is removed from public
    display; where no legal retention obligation applies, it is also erased
    from the operator's records. The identifier persists with status
    retracted and a non-identifying tombstone record. The minimum retained
    record contains the identifier, status, and registration date; titles or
    other potentially identifying metadata are retained only where lawful.
    This trade-off between privacy and identifier persistence is deliberate
    and disclosed.
  - Optional AI-provenance fields (AI system version, training-data
    references) raise confidentiality (commercial sensitivity) rather than
    privacy concerns; their publication is at the holder's discretion, and
    the public database provides field-level visibility controls.

Interoperability
----------------

  - aibn and isbn are distinct NIDs; there is no syntactic conflict within
    the URN framework. The similarity of the strings is a human-factors
    risk, addressed in the NID justification (Additional Information).
    Systems SHOULD display the full urn:aibn: prefix and SHOULD NOT
    abbreviate the NID.
  - AIBN records MAY reference ISBNs; the two systems identify resources at
    different granularities (ISBN: manifestation; AIBN: work).
  - ISNI [ISO27729] and ORCID identify parties; AIBN identifies works. The
    metadata schema includes optional ISNI/ORCID fields for responsible
    parties; the Registry Operator does not issue ISNIs.
  - DOI [ISO26324] and Handle are persistent-identifier systems outside the
    URN framework. The same work MAY carry both a DOI and an AIBN;
    cross-system resolution is a matter for the respective resolvers and is
    not defined here.
  - ARK identifiers overlap functionally; AIBN's distinguishing features are
    as stated under Purpose.

Resolution
----------

Status: Resolution services have not yet been deployed. Resolution will be
provided by the public HTTPS lookup API described under Assignment (Metadata
and validation); no separate resolution infrastructure is required. The IANA
registration record will be updated if additional resolution mechanisms are
added.

Mechanism:

  - HTTPS resolution: the Registry Operator will operate one or more public
    HTTPS endpoints (URLs published in the Registration Rules) that accept
    an AIBN and return the public metadata record. This is the same service
    as the validation lookup API described under Assignment; validation and
    resolution differ in intent, not implementation. The endpoint will
    accept a complete AIBN URN or its NSS as a lookup key. Exact endpoint
    URL, request syntax, HTTP status codes for unknown and withdrawn
    identifiers, and error representations MUST be published in the
    Registration Rules before public service launch. The default
    representation is JSON-LD; MARCXML and plain text may be requested
    through HTTP content negotiation or an endpoint-local format=marcxml or
    format=text URL query parameter. These endpoint parameters are not URN
    q- or r-components. The JSON-LD schema is published and versioned (see
    Documentation).
  - Service discovery: no DNS-based urn.arpa DDDS or NAPTR records are
    required for this namespace registration, and none are currently
    proposed. The authoritative HTTPS service URL will be published in the
    Registration Rules. A separate DNS-based discovery registration (for
    example, under applicable DDDS/NAPTR procedures) may be pursued in the
    future if warranted, subject to the relevant separate registration
    procedures; this is an option, not a commitment.
  - Mirrors: third parties MAY mirror the bulk database export and operate
    resolvers; criteria for recognized resolvers (freshness requirements,
    provenance verification) will be published. The Registry Operator's
    endpoint remains authoritative.
  - r-component: no special semantics (see Syntax).

Additional Information
----------------------

NID confusability justification: aibn is similar to the registered NID isbn.
The similarity is intentional, and the parallel is explicit in the
namespace's designation: both namespaces provide bibliographic
identification, and AI Bibliographic Name deliberately echoes International
Standard Book Number. The designations also differ at a documentable point —
ISBN identifiers are fixed-length, digits-only numbers assigned per
manifestation, whereas AIBNs are variable-length alphanumeric names assigned
per work, which supports distinguishing the two in prose and documentation.
Precedent: isbn and issn are both registered NIDs differing by a single
character; any resulting human confusion has been managed without renaming.
Mitigation: full-prefix display (urn:aibn:) is recommended, and the two
namespaces will never share assignment databases or resolution
infrastructure.

Comparison with ISBN (informative): It is not the case that ISBN
categorically excludes AI-generated works; such works have obtained ISBNs
through self-publishing channels. The gaps AIBN addresses are (a) absence of
AI-provenance metadata, (b) assignment tied to the publishing supply chain,
and (c) restrictive policies in some channels. AIBN is a supplement to, not
a replacement for, ISBN.

Identifier capacity (informative):

  - Per-holder space: each publication-segment consists of 1–63 characters
    using lowercase alphanumeric characters, with hyphens permitted
    internally. Each alphanumeric character carries ≈ 5.17 bits of
    identifier space versus ≈ 3.32 for a decimal digit. Six-character
    lowercase alphanumeric publication-segments alone provide 36⁶ ≈ 2.2 ×
    10⁹ distinct values per holder (a conservative subset of the full
    syntax) — on the order of the entire ISBN-13 space under its current 978
    and 979 GS1 prefixes (approximately 2 × 10⁹); ten characters provide
    36¹⁰ ≈ 3.7 × 10¹⁵.
  - Namespace-level space: overall capacity is the product of the number of
    assigned reg-segments and each holder's local publication space. The
    reg-segment space is drawn from the same 36-symbol, 63-character
    alphabet; even four-character lowercase alphanumeric reg-segments alone
    would accommodate 36⁴ ≈ 1.7 million holders. The combined space is, for
    practical purposes, unbounded.
  - No serialization bottleneck: because assignment is hierarchical (see
    Assignment), holders mint publication-segments locally and in parallel.
    The Registry Operator's involvement is required once per holder, not
    once per identifier, so growth in publication volume does not constrain
    identifier availability.
  - Granularity: AIBN assigns one identifier per work (see Assignment,
    Granularity). Systems that assign per manifestation consume identifiers
    in proportion to the number of published formats of each work; AIBN's
    identifier consumption scales with works created rather than formats
    distributed.
  - Consistency with the designation: publication-segments are not
    restricted to digits; holders MAY assign numeric, mnemonic, or opaque
    values (see Syntax). This flexibility is consistent with the designation
    "Name" rather than "Number."
  - Scope of these comparisons: no claim of near-term exhaustion of the ISBN
    space is made or implied. The comparisons above are illustrative of
    per-holder and namespace capacity; the ISBN system retains substantial
    headroom and established extension mechanisms (e.g., additional GS1
    prefixes, as demonstrated by the addition of 979 alongside 978). AIBN's
    rationale (see Purpose) does not depend on ISBN capacity.
  - Demand context: The volume of self-published books has grown
    substantially, creating increasing demand for scalable publication and
    identifier-assignment infrastructure. According to Bowker's ISBN-based
    U.S. publishing statistics, reported by Publishers Weekly on 17 March
    2026, the number of self-published titles increased from 2,459,367 in
    2022 to 3,529,980 in 2025, representing 43.5% growth over three years,
    including a 38.7% increase between 2024 and 2025 alone (Jim Milliot,
    "Book Output Topped Four Million in 2025
    (https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/99943-book-output-topped-4-million-in-2025.html),"
    Publishers Weekly, 17 March 2026). These figures count ISBN assignments
    and therefore manifestations rather than works; work-level volume may
    differ. Alongside this expansion, the proliferation of AI-assisted
    publishing has raised concerns about automated, high-volume submissions,
    prompting platforms to introduce publication restrictions. In September
    2023, Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) introduced a limit of three
    new titles per publisher per day as a measure against potential abuse,
    although Amazon stated that it had not observed an overall spike in
    publication volume at that time (Jim Milliot, "Amazon's Kindle Direct
    Publishing Will Limit Daily Number of New Titles
    (https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/93207-kdp-will-limit-daily-number-of-new-titles.html),"
    Publishers Weekly, 18 September 2023). Amazon's subsequently revised KDP
    publication guidelines
    (https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G202172740) specify a limit of
    two new titles per format per week (accessed 16 October 2026). Although
    these statistics do not establish what proportion of publication growth
    is attributable to AI-generated content, they demonstrate the increasing
    scale of self-publishing and the emergence of platform-level controls on
    submission volumes. The identifier-assignment architecture is designed
    to accommodate continued growth in publication volume without imposing
    corresponding constraints on identifier availability, decoupling
    identifier capacity from platform-specific publication limits and the
    rate of new content generation.

Legal status: Registration creates no rights and makes no representation of
authorship or ownership; an AIBN is solely a technical identifier.

Governance: The Registry Operator maintains the Registration Rules and
Metadata Schema. Material changes (assignment mechanism, uniqueness policy,
resolution requirements) are published for public comment for at least 30
days before taking effect and recorded in the change log. Changes to the
IANA registration record are filed per the namespace-change procedures of
[RFC8141].

Reserved segments: the reg-segment values example, test, invalid, and
reserved are permanently reserved for documentation, examples, and testing,
and will never be assigned.

Typo resistance: reg-segments are assigned as opaque codes with no embedded
meaning. A check-character scheme was considered and is not used in version
1; mistyped AIBNs are expected to fail lookup against the authoritative
database.
