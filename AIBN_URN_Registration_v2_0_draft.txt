Purpose
-------

This namespace provides persistent, location-independent identifiers (AIBNs)
for works created in whole or in part with the assistance of generative AI
systems, for use by individuals, publishers, and organizations to designate
such works. An AIBN is a technical identifier; registration of an AIBN by an
entity for a work does not imply or depend on the entity possessing any
particular right to the work (see Non-effect on rights, below).

Terminology: the "Registry Operator" is the URN registrant named in
Registration Information; a "holder" is a party to which the Registry
Operator has assigned a reg-segment (see Assignment).

[EDITORIAL NOTE 2: item 19. This sentence replaces the Terminology bullet
that used to sit late in Privacy Considerations, so that "holder" is defined
before its first use instead of nine uses too late. If you prefer a

Designation. AIBN expands to AI Bibliographic Name. "Bibliographic" is used
in its information-science sense and covers resources of any medium, not
only text. "Name" rather than "Number" reflects that an AIBN is a structured
name — alphanumeric, variable-length, and assignable by holders as numeric
sequences, mnemonics, or opaque codes (see Syntax) — in keeping with the
"Name" in Uniform Resource Name [RFC8141].

AIBN is a complement to ISBN, not a replacement. ISBN (ISO 2108) identifies
monographic publications at manifestation level and is assigned through the
International ISBN Agency network and the publishing supply chain. Works
involving AI generation can in some channels obtain ISBNs; however, the ISBN
metadata model has no fields for AI provenance (AI system and version,
degree of involvement) or for a responsible human party distinct from an
"author," and not all registration channels accommodate such works. AIBN
provides an identification layer that records this provenance persistently.
A single work may carry an ISBN, a DOI, and an AIBN simultaneously.

The concept of a "work". Throughout this registration, a "work" is the
intellectual or artistic content of a creation produced in whole or in
part with the assistance of generative AI systems, identified
independently of any particular manifestation, embodiment, or location of
that content. A novel, its e-book edition, and its audiobook recording
are, in this sense, manifestations of one work; so are a generated
image, its digital files, and its print reproductions; an AIBN names the
work, not its manifestations, embodiments, or locations. The concept is
similar in spirit to the concept of a "work" in copyright law, but it is
used here in a deliberately media-neutral and law-neutral way: AIBN
assignment is not conditioned on copyright subsistence (see Non-effect
on rights), and nothing in this registration depends on any particular
legal system's definition of a "work".

Eligibility. To qualify for identification, a work must have crossed the
threshold from computational process to finalized product. Four
requirements apply:

1. Fixation. The work has been rendered, in at least one form, into a
   stable, distributable format. Fixation is a condition of eligibility,
   not the object of identification: an AIBN names the work, not any
   particular fixed form of it (manifestations are identified by
   format-level systems such as ISBN; see Granularity, under Assignment).

2. Human agency at design or generation time. The work embodies more
   than minimal human creative direction — either direct direction of
   the generation (deliberate prompt architecture, parameter or model
   selection, post-generation editing, curation of generated material)
   or the deliberate design of the process that produced it (pipeline
   architecture, model selection and configuration, acceptance rules).
   Works produced by unattended pipelines are eligible when the pipeline
   is itself a deliberately designed process; the pipeline's operator is
   named as responsible party (see Assignment, Metadata and validation).
   This is a factual, process-level criterion; it is not a judgment of
   creativity or authorship and has no bearing on copyright status (see
   Non-effect on rights).

3. Discreteness. The work is a discrete, monographic unit of consumption,
   with a defined beginning and end, rather than an unbounded or
   open-ended stream, session, or feed. A continuing resource (e.g., a
   periodical or a broadcast channel) is not itself a work under this
   definition; its individually finalized issues, episodes, or pieces may
   each be a work. Continuing resources as such are the domain of the
   issn namespace.

4. Human-consumable experience. The work is designed to be read, heard,
   viewed, or interacted with by an audience, rather than serving as a
   backend or machine-consumed utility (data feeds, API responses,
   intermediate representations).

Accordingly, the namespace excludes outputs that are transient,
unbounded, or purely functional. These exclusions are stated as
characteristics of the work rather than as a list of formats, so that
the scope remains meaningful as technologies change. The granularity at
which content counts as one work is declared by the registering holder
and is guided — but not mechanically determined — by the rules in
Granularity (under Assignment).

Circulation. The namespace serves works that reach an audience. A work
is expected to be made available — or intended for eventual
availability — to persons other than the responsible party itself; the
AIBN system is a registry of works in circulation, not a private
numbering scheme. Consistent with format-and-completeness validation,
this expectation is not verified at recording time; it is applied ex
post, on an evident pattern, under the Registration Rules.

Why existing namespaces do not suffice: no existing NID or
persistent-identifier system combines (1) work-level identification, (2)
open assignment to any natural person or legal entity — not conditioned on
copyright subsistence, publisher status, or supply-chain participation, (3)
a metadata model with a mandatory responsible-party field and optional
AI-provenance fields, and (4) URN-framework persistence and resolution
semantics. isbn is manifestation-level and supply-chain-administered; issn
covers serials; DOI, Handle, and ARK are persistent-identifier systems
outside the URN framework with their own assignment policies; urn:uuid
provides uniqueness without an assignment authority, metadata, or
resolution: UUIDs are minted locally by whoever needs one (RFC 9562
requires no central registration process), so no registry records which
identifiers exist or what they name.

Non-effect on rights: An AIBN is a technical identifier. Registration does
not create, imply, transfer, or substitute for copyright, related rights,
moral rights, or legal personality, and does not constitute a determination
of authorship or ownership. The copyright status of a work is determined
solely by applicable law and is independent of AIBN assignment.

Syntax
------

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
"SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
"OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14
[RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as
shown here.

The Namespace Specific String (NSS) has the following syntax (ABNF per
[RFC5234]):

AIBN-NSS    = reg-segment ":" pub-segment
reg-segment = segment
pub-segment = segment
segment     = ext-char / 2ext-char / ext-char 1*61seg-char ext-char
seg-char    = ext-char / "-"
ext-char    = %x61-7A / DIGIT   ; lowercase ASCII letter or digit

[EDITORIAL NOTE 4: item 6. Strict grammar as proposed by the reviewer, with
two judgment calls: (a) "2ext-char" (exactly two) replaces the reviewer's
"2*ext-char" (two or more, unbounded), keeping the 63-character cap
airtight; (b) under this grammar uppercase is syntactically invalid, which
resolves the old "grammar permits uppercase" contradiction — Lexical
equivalence, the Security bullet, and the Length paragraph below are
aligned accordingly (the 128-character cap is now derivable as 127).]

This grammar states the lexical constraints directly: each segment is 1 to
63 characters long, begins and ends with an ext-char (a lowercase letter or
digit), and contains only seg-char (lowercase letters, digits, and hyphen)
in between; exactly one ":" separates the two segments; the alphabet is a
subset of unreserved ([RFC3986] §2.3). Uppercase letters are not part of
the alphabet: an AIBN is always lowercase, and a URN whose NSS contains
uppercase characters is not a valid AIBN URN. "%" is likewise not part of
the alphabet, so percent-encoding cannot appear in a valid AIBN NSS;
because NSS comparison is octet-wise, a URN containing percent-encoded
octets (e.g., %61 for a) never matches an assigned AIBN.

Length: the grammar limits the NSS to at most 127 characters (each segment
at most 63, plus the separator). The q-, r-, and f-components (below) are
additional to this limit. Software generating URNs with such components
SHOULD keep the complete URN within 512 characters for compatibility with
common URI-handling implementations.

Component handling:

  - q-component: permitted; not part of AIBN identity; passed to the
    resolution service. The q-component is introduced by ?= under [RFC8141].
    AIBN defines no namespace-specific q-component parameters at initial
    registration. HTTP clients MAY use the endpoint-specific format query
    parameter as described under Resolution; this is a URL query parameter,
    not an AIBN URN component.
  - r-component: introduced by ?+ under [RFC8141]; no namespace-specific
    semantics are defined, and its use is not recommended pending
    standardization of r-component semantics.
  - f-component: as per [RFC3986]/[RFC8141]; interpreted client-side; not
    part of identity.

Lexical equivalence:

  - The "urn" scheme and NID are compared case-insensitively, per URN
    syntax.
  - The NSS is defined in lowercase only and is compared case-sensitively,
    octet-wise. Two URNs denote the same AIBN if and only if their schemes
    and NIDs match case-insensitively and their NSSs are octet-identical.
  - q-, r-, and f-components are ignored for identity and equivalence.

Semantics:

  - reg-segment: an opaque registrant code identifying an AIBN holder. It is
    unique across the entire aibn namespace and is assigned by the Registry
    Operator. It carries no embedded meaning.
  - pub-segment: an identifier, unique within its reg-segment, for a single
    work (see The concept of a "work", under Purpose, and Granularity,
    under Assignment). It is assigned by the holder, which MAY use numeric
    sequences, mnemonics, or opaque codes; the syntax imposes no structure.
  - The identified resource is a specific work (see The concept of a
    "work", under Purpose).

Examples (the reg-segment "example" is permanently reserved and can never
be assigned):

urn:aibn:example:2026-0001
urn:aibn:example:first-light-7a

Apparent patterns in pub-segments (dates, sequence numbers, words) are
conventions of the holder with no syntax-defined semantics; consumers MUST
NOT parse segments for meaning unless they have specific knowledge of the
assignment policies of the holder of a particular reg-segment.

Assignment
----------

Assignment model (hierarchical):

  - The Registry Operator is the sole authority for reg-segments. It assigns
    them on application, guarantees their global uniqueness, and never
    reassigns a reg-segment — including after a holder becomes inactive or
    dissolves. The reg-segment assignment function is not delegated to third
    parties.
  - Within its reg-segment, a holder assigns pub-segments and is responsible
    for their uniqueness within that reg-segment. The holder MUST record each
    pub-segment in the AIBN database at or before its first public use; the
    database rejects duplicates within a reg-segment.
  - The combined NSS is therefore globally unique.

Identifier persistence:

  - An assigned AIBN is never reused or reassigned under any circumstances.
  - If a work is deleted, withdrawn, or retracted, the AIBN remains valid;
    the database status changes (see below) and at minimum the identifier,
    status, and registration date are retained; the title is retained only
    insofar as applicable privacy and other law permits.
  - Metadata corrections are recorded with a change log; the identifier
    itself never changes.
  - Lifecycle statuses: active (default), retracted (withdrawn by the
    holder, or removed by the Registry Operator for policy violation),
    superseded (replaced by a new AIBN for a substantially new version, with
    an optional cross-reference). No status transition ever frees an
    identifier for reassignment.

Granularity: An AIBN identifies one work at creation level (see The concept
of a "work", under Purpose). A substantially revised or new edition SHOULD
receive a new AIBN, optionally cross-linked via supersedes/superseded-by.
Minor corrections, formatting changes, and reissue in a new medium or format
do not receive a new AIBN and are not recorded by the registry. (Contrast:
ISBN is assigned per manifestation.) Manifestations of a work are identified
by format-level systems such as ISBN and MAY be cross-referenced in AIBN
metadata; current availability is a property of resolution targets, not of
the registry record.

Openness and criteria: Assignment is open. Any natural person of full
legal capacity, or any legal entity, may obtain a reg-segment upon
application, subject to published criteria: (a) acceptance of the
Registration Rules [AIBN-RULES]; (b) provision of a private, verifiable
contact channel. No fees are charged at launch; any future fees will be
published in advance in the Registration Rules. Assignment is not
conditioned on copyright subsistence, publication status, nationality,
or membership in any publishing organization. Multiple
AIBNs MAY be assigned to the same work by different parties; the Registry
Operator does not adjudicate such disputes, and metadata MAY cross-reference
other identifiers for the same work (e.g., ISBN, DOI) or for the same
responsible party or other persons named in the record (e.g., ISNI, ORCID).

Sponsored recording: A holder MAY record pub-segments for works of third
parties that are not holders (sponsored recording, see Registration
Rules [AIBN-RULES]); the third party MAY be named as responsible party.
This does not delegate the reg-segment assignment function: the Registry
Operator remains the sole authority for reg-segments, and a sponsored
third party that later becomes a holder receives its own reg-segment
under the ordinary application procedure. The Registry Operator MAY
itself act as a sponsoring holder; reg-segments held by the Registry
Operator are identified as operator-held in the public documentation.

Metadata and validation:

  Responsible party: the natural person or legal entity named in an AIBN
  record that stands behind the registration. The responsible party declares
  the record's metadata and serves as the accountability and contact point
  for it; "responsible" refers to responsibility for the accuracy of the
  declared metadata and for responding to contact about the registration —
  it does not imply authorship of, or any legal or moral right in, the work
  (see Non-effect on rights). The responsible party may be, but need not
  be, the work's author or rights holder, and may be the holder that
  assigned the pub-segment, a party acting at its direction, or a party
  on whose behalf the holder records the work (see Sponsored recording,
  above).

  - Minimum metadata at registration: work title; work type (a content type
    from the controlled vocabulary published in the Metadata Schema
    [AIBN-SCHEMA], e.g., text, image, audio, audiovisual, software,
    dataset, multimodal, other); creation date; responsible natural person
    or entity; private contact channel; degree of AI involvement
    (self-declared value from a controlled vocabulary published in the
    Metadata Schema [AIBN-SCHEMA], e.g., human-with-ai-tools, ai-assisted,
    ai-generated, autonomous), which is required and always published — it
    is the record's core provenance declaration and cannot be elected
    non-public. Optional: pen name for public display; form of work (e.g.,
    novel, report, image series); language (BCP 47 [RFC5646] tag, e.g.,
    en, fr-CA, zh-Hans; more than one MAY be given for multilingual
    works); description (short abstract); creation carrier (MIME type of
    the form in which the work was first fixed, e.g., text/markdown); AI
    system name and version; references to identifiers for the same work
    or its manifestations (e.g., ISBN, DOI); references to identifiers for
    persons named in the record, such as the responsible party or a
    pen-named author (e.g., ISNI, ORCID). Of the optional fields, only the
    description and the AI system name and version may be recorded without
    publication (field-level visibility, Registration Rules [AIBN-RULES]);
    the remaining optional fields are published when present.
    Manifestation data (formats, editions, availability) is intentionally
    absent from the schema; see Granularity.
  - All metadata is as supplied by the holder. In addition, the registry
    itself records the registration date and the lifecycle status of the
    AIBN (active, retracted, or superseded; see Identifier persistence),
    which are assigned and maintained by the Registry Operator and cannot
    be altered by the holder. The Registry Operator validates format and
    completeness only; it does not verify authorship, ownership, or factual
    accuracy, and states this in all outputs. The Registry Operator MAY
    refuse or retract registrations that are malformed or abusive; an
    appeals process is defined in the Registration Rules [AIBN-RULES].
  - Third-party validation is supported by (a) a public HTTPS lookup API
    returning the public metadata record, with documented rate limits, and
    (b) a complete bulk export of the public database (daily snapshots plus
    an append-only change log). The authoritative database is the sole
    source of truth for validity; this is the mechanism for detecting forged
    or unauthorized AIBNs.

Continuity: The Registry Operator will maintain a data-escrow arrangement
with an independent escrow agent and will maintain a succession plan. The
details of both will be published in the Registration Rules [AIBN-RULES]. If
operation ceases without a successor, the escrow agent will be responsible
for publishing the escrowed database in static form so identifiers remain
verifiable, and the IANA registration record will be updated per the
procedures of [RFC8141] (e.g., to mark resolution inactive). Identifiers
remain syntactically valid URNs regardless of resolution status.

Security and Privacy Considerations
-----------------------------------

Security:

  - AIBNs are identifiers, not credentials. They MUST NOT be used for
    authentication, authorization, or access control, and MUST NOT be
    treated as secrets or capability tokens.
  - Implementations comparing AIBNs MUST compare the NSS octet-wise and the
    scheme and NID case-insensitively (see Lexical Equivalence). The
    lowercase-only alphabet eliminates most case-mismatch errors in
    practice.
  - Identifiers are opaque and MUST NOT be constructed to embed personal or
    sensitive data.
  - The authoritative database (lookup API and bulk export) is the only
    validity oracle; resolvers SHOULD resolve against it or a verified
    mirror.

Privacy:

  - The public record never requires a natural person's real name; a pen
    name or organization name MAY be displayed instead. A private,
    verifiable contact channel is required at registration but is not
    published.
  - The Registry Operator acts as data controller for personal data it
    processes and complies with the General Data Protection Regulation (EU)
    2016/679 (GDPR). Data is collected minimally; the retention policy is
    published in the Registration Rules.
  - On a valid deletion request, personal data is removed from public
    display; where no legal retention obligation applies, it is also erased
    from the operator's records. The identifier persists with status
    retracted and a non-identifying tombstone record. The minimum retained
    record contains the identifier, status, and registration date; titles or
    other potentially identifying metadata are retained only where lawful.
    This trade-off between privacy and identifier persistence is deliberate
    and disclosed.
  - The optional AI-system field (system name and version) raises
    confidentiality (commercial sensitivity) rather than privacy concerns;
    its publication is at the holder's discretion, and the public database
    provides field-level visibility controls (Registration Rules
    [AIBN-RULES]). The degree of AI involvement, by contrast, is required
    and always published: a single value from a small controlled
    vocabulary, it is the record's core provenance declaration rather
    than tool detail.

Interoperability
----------------

  - aibn and isbn are distinct NIDs; there is no syntactic conflict within
    the URN framework. The similarity of the strings is a human-factors
    risk, addressed in the NID justification (Additional Information).
    Systems SHOULD display the full urn:aibn: prefix and SHOULD NOT
    abbreviate the NID.
  - AIBN records MAY reference ISBNs; the two systems identify resources at
    different granularities (ISBN: manifestation; AIBN: work).
  - ISNI [ISO27729] and ORCID identify parties; AIBN identifies works. The
    metadata schema includes optional ISNI/ORCID fields for responsible
    parties; the Registry Operator does not issue ISNIs.
  - DOI [ISO26324] and Handle are persistent-identifier systems outside the
    URN framework. The same work MAY carry both a DOI and an AIBN;
    cross-system resolution is a matter for the respective resolvers and is
    not defined here.
  - ARK identifiers overlap functionally; AIBN's distinguishing features are
    as stated under Purpose.

Resolution
----------

Status: Resolution services have not yet been deployed. Resolution will be
provided by the public HTTPS lookup API described under Assignment (Metadata
and validation); no separate resolution infrastructure is required. The IANA
registration record will be updated if additional resolution mechanisms are
added.

Mechanism:

  - HTTPS resolution: the Registry Operator will operate one or more public
    HTTPS endpoints (URLs published in the Registration Rules) that accept
    an AIBN and return the public metadata record. This is the same service
    as the validation lookup API described under Assignment; validation and
    resolution differ in intent, not implementation. The endpoint will
    accept a complete AIBN URN or its NSS as a lookup key. Exact endpoint
    URL, request syntax, HTTP status codes for unknown and withdrawn
    identifiers, and error representations MUST be published in the
    Registration Rules. The default representation is JSON-LD; MARCXML and
    plain text may be requested through HTTP content negotiation or an
    endpoint-local format=marcxml or format=text URL query parameter. These
    endpoint parameters are not URN q- or r-components. The JSON-LD schema
    is published and versioned (see Documentation).
  - Service discovery: no DNS-based urn.arpa DDDS or NAPTR records are
    required for this namespace registration, and none are currently
    proposed. The authoritative HTTPS service URL will be published in the
    Registration Rules. A separate DNS-based discovery registration (for
    example, under applicable DDDS/NAPTR procedures) may be pursued in the
    future if warranted, subject to the relevant separate registration
    procedures; this is an option, not a commitment.
  - Mirrors: third parties MAY mirror the bulk database export and operate
    resolvers; criteria for recognized resolvers (freshness requirements,
    provenance verification) will be published. The Registry Operator's
    endpoint remains authoritative.
  - r-component: no special semantics (see Syntax).

Additional Information
----------------------

NID confusability justification: aibn is similar to the registered NID isbn.
The similarity is intentional, and the parallel is explicit in the
namespace's designation: both namespaces provide bibliographic
identification, and AI Bibliographic Name deliberately echoes International
Standard Book Number. The designations also differ at a documentable point —
ISBN identifiers are fixed-length, digits-only numbers assigned per
manifestation, whereas AIBNs are variable-length alphanumeric names assigned
per work, which supports distinguishing the two in prose and documentation.
Precedent: isbn and issn are both registered NIDs differing by a single
character; any resulting human confusion has been managed without renaming.
Mitigation: full-prefix display (urn:aibn:) is recommended — ISBNs,
similarly, are conventionally displayed with an "ISBN" prefix — and the two
namespaces will never share assignment databases or resolution
infrastructure.

Comparison with ISBN (informative): It is not the case that ISBN
categorically excludes AI-generated works; such works have obtained ISBNs
through self-publishing channels. The gaps AIBN addresses are, foremost,
(a) scale: the AIBN namespace is many orders of magnitude larger than the
ISBN-13 address space (approximately 2 × 10⁹ values under the current 978
and 979 GS1 prefixes) — a single holder's six-character publication-segment
sub-space alone approximates the entire ISBN-13 space (see Identifier
capacity, below) — and is sized to accommodate the observed and projected
growth of AI-assisted works and of the population eligible to register
them. The further gaps are (b) absence of AI-provenance metadata, (c)
assignment tied to the publishing supply chain, and (d) restrictive
policies in some channels. AIBN is a supplement to, not a replacement for,
ISBN.

Identifier capacity (informative):

  - Per-holder space: each publication-segment consists of 1–63 characters
    using lowercase alphanumeric characters, with hyphens permitted
    internally. Each alphanumeric character carries ≈ 5.17 bits of
    identifier space versus ≈ 3.32 for a decimal digit. Six-character
    lowercase alphanumeric publication-segments alone provide 36⁶ ≈ 2.2 ×
    10⁹ distinct values per holder (a conservative subset of the full
    syntax) — on the order of the entire ISBN-13 space under its current 978
    and 979 GS1 prefixes (approximately 2 × 10⁹); ten characters provide
    36¹⁰ ≈ 3.7 × 10¹⁵.
  - Namespace-level space: overall capacity is the product of the number of
    assigned reg-segments and each holder's local publication space. The
    reg-segment space is drawn from the same 36-symbol, 63-character
    alphabet; even four-character lowercase alphanumeric reg-segments alone
    would accommodate 36⁴ ≈ 1.7 million holders. The combined space is, for
    practical purposes, unbounded.
  - No serialization bottleneck: because assignment is hierarchical (see
    Assignment), holders mint publication-segments locally and in parallel.
    The Registry Operator's involvement is required once per holder, not
    once per identifier, so growth in publication volume does not constrain
    identifier availability.
  - Granularity: AIBN assigns one identifier per work (see Assignment,
    Granularity). Systems that assign per manifestation consume identifiers
    in proportion to the number of published formats of each work; AIBN's
    identifier consumption scales with works created rather than formats
    distributed.
  - Consistency with the designation: publication-segments are not
    restricted to digits; holders MAY assign numeric, mnemonic, or opaque
    values (see Syntax). This flexibility is consistent with the designation
    "Name" rather than "Number."
  - Scope of these comparisons: no claim of near-term exhaustion of the ISBN
    space is made or implied. The comparisons above — including the scale
    gap under Comparison with ISBN — are illustrative of per-holder and
    namespace capacity; the ISBN system retains substantial headroom and
    established extension mechanisms (e.g., additional GS1 prefixes, as
    demonstrated by the addition of 979 alongside 978). AIBN's rationale
    (see Purpose) rests on the kinds of works and registrants it serves and
    on their observed and projected volume, not on any claim about ISBN's
    remaining headroom.
  - Demand context: The volume of self-published books has grown
    substantially, creating increasing demand for scalable publication and
    identifier-assignment infrastructure. According to Bowker's ISBN-based
    U.S. publishing statistics, reported by Publishers Weekly on 17 March
    2026, the number of self-published titles increased from 2,459,367 in
    2022 to 3,529,980 in 2025, representing 43.5% growth over three years,
    including a 38.7% increase between 2024 and 2025 alone (Jim Milliot,
    "Book Output Topped Four Million in 2025
    (https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/99943-book-output-topped-4-million-in-2025.html),"
    Publishers Weekly, 17 March 2026). These figures count ISBN assignments
    and therefore manifestations rather than works; work-level volume may
    differ. Alongside this expansion, the proliferation of AI-assisted
    publishing has raised concerns about automated, high-volume submissions,
    prompting platforms to introduce publication restrictions. In September
    2023, Amazon Kindle Direct Publishing (KDP) introduced a limit of three
    new titles per publisher per day as a measure against potential abuse,
    although Amazon stated that it had not observed an overall spike in
    publication volume at that time (Jim Milliot, "Amazon's Kindle Direct
    Publishing Will Limit Daily Number of New Titles
    (https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/93207-kdp-will-limit-daily-number-of-new-titles.html),"
    Publishers Weekly, 18 September 2023). Amazon's subsequently revised KDP
    publication guidelines
    (https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G202172740) specify a limit of
    two new titles per format per week (accessed 16 October 2026). Although
    these statistics do not establish what proportion of publication growth
    is attributable to AI-generated content, they demonstrate the increasing
    scale of self-publishing and the emergence of platform-level controls on
    submission volumes. The identifier-assignment architecture is designed
    to accommodate continued growth in publication volume without imposing
    corresponding constraints on identifier availability, decoupling
    identifier capacity from platform-specific publication limits and the
    rate of new content generation.

Legal status: Registration creates no rights and makes no representation of
authorship or ownership; an AIBN is solely a technical identifier.

Governance: The Registry Operator maintains the Registration Rules and
Metadata Schema. Material changes (assignment mechanism, uniqueness policy,
resolution requirements) are published for public comment for at least 30
days before taking effect and recorded in the change log. Changes to the
IANA registration record are filed per the namespace-change procedures of
[RFC8141].

Reserved segments: the reg-segment values "example", "test", "invalid",
and "reserved" are permanently reserved for documentation, examples, and
testing, and will never be assigned.

Typo resistance: reg-segments are assigned as opaque codes with no embedded
meaning. A check-character scheme was considered and is not used in version
1; mistyped AIBNs are expected to fail lookup against the authoritative
database.
